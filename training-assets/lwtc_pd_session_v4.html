Faculty and Staff Professional Development Session

Differentiation and Reflection — 60 minutes — LWTC

Facilitator view:

A 60-minute session moving from differentiation into reflection. Both blocks are active — no slides, no passive listening. Your experience is the primary resource in the room.

FacilitatorBefore you begin

All faculty and staff completed the DI Canvas discussion. Department chairs are seated with their departments today — they bring prior context on the reflection research from the chairs meeting, which will surface naturally in pair conversations without needing to be staged.

Pull one or two patterns from the Canvas discussion replies before the session starts. Your synthesis of what participants wrote is your five-minute opener for Block 1 — no slides needed.

Identify the one pattern that surprised you most, and the one that appeared most consistently. Those two observations are your entire opener. Let the room respond briefly, then move directly into the cards.

Differentiation: from discussion to decision

Segment 2 of 3 · learner profile cards
10 min10:00
Small group application
As a group, choose one learner profile card below. Apply one DI strategy from the Canvas discussion to that scenario. There is no single right answer — the goal is a specific, concrete response.
How to choose your card: Read the program label and "best fit" line on each card before opening it. Pick the one that most closely matches your department or role. If two feel equally close, choose the one that makes your group slightly more uncomfortable — that's usually the more useful one.
Health sciences
Best fit: PN, PCT, Phlebotomy, Surgical Tech, RN
The experienced CNA returning for clinical hours
Process
Tap to open full scenario ↓

A Practical Nursing student with 8 years of CNA experience sits through a unit on patient communication she feels she already knows well enough to teach. She disengages during lecture but excels in clinical settings.

Knowles connection: Experience and self-concept. Her prior knowledge is both an asset and a source of frustration when it goes unrecognized.

Your group task: How would you differentiate the process so her experience is used as a resource rather than bypassed? What does that look like in a clinical or simulation setting?

"One way we could use her experience as a resource is ____."

Trades and technical
Best fit: HVAC/R, Building Construction, ESIA, Marine, Automotive
The industry veteran who reads below grade level
Content
Tap to open full scenario ↓

An HVAC/R student with 12 years in the field struggles with written exam prep materials. He can diagnose a refrigerant leak by sound but cannot reliably decode the textbook section on the same topic.

Knowles connection: Learning profile and orientation. He is internally motivated and highly capable — the barrier is the access point, not the concept.

Your group task: How would you differentiate the content so it meets him where he is? What is one realistic alternative access point for the same material?

"A realistic alternative access point for this content might be ____."

Business and technology
Best fit: Digital Media, CSIT, DBAP, Pharmacy Tech, Dental, Medical Assisting
The first-generation student who doesn't see the relevance
Product
Tap to open full scenario ↓

A Business Technology student attends every class but submits minimal work. When asked, she says the assignments feel disconnected from anything she would actually do in a job. She has a part-time job in retail and a clear financial goal.

Knowles connection: Readiness and orientation. She needs to see the immediate application — the "why this, why now" — before she invests effort.

Your group task: How would you differentiate the product so the demonstration of learning connects to something real in her life? What choices could you build in?

"One way to make the product feel immediately relevant to her is ____."

Advising and support staff
Best fit: Workforce Education Advisors, Financial Aid Advisors
The advisor stretched across three programs
Process
Tap to open full scenario ↓

A Workforce Education Advisor supports students in Dental, Pharmacy Tech, and Medical Assisting. She attends the same group workshops as all other faculty, but her daily work — caseloads, district metrics, no classroom — looks nothing like the instructors around her.

Knowles connection: Need to know and self-concept. She wants to see how PD applies to her role specifically, not to teaching a class she doesn't have.

Your group task: How would you differentiate the process in a shared PD experience so that advising staff can engage authentically rather than translating everything? What might that look like today, in this room?

"In this session, advisors could engage with the content differently by ____."

FacilitatorMove through the room while groups work. If a group finishes early, push deeper: "What would that look like on day one of the unit, not just in theory?"

Block 2 0:20 – 1:00 · 40 minutes
Reflection: the research case
You'll encounter the same research your department chairs worked through — which means by the end of this block, everyone in the room has a shared starting point for the conversation about planning and reflection.
Segment 1 of 4
5 min5:00
Framing the research
Your facilitator will share one key finding from the research before you read and watch. Look for it as you go through the resources in the next segment.

FacilitatorScript (three sentences only): "Your department chairs have been working with a piece of research that changed how they think about planning. Today you get the same starting point. Here's the one finding I want you to look for as you read and watch: a group of trainees who spent 15 minutes reflecting at the end of each day outperformed a group that kept working — by about 23%. We're going to test that against your own experience."

Three sentences. No more. Send them to their devices.

Writing prompt — individual

Segment 4 of 4
10 min10:00
Pair or trio conversation
Share your written response with your partner — about 3 minutes each. Then move together to the second question below.

Second question for the pair: Where does reflection currently live in your instructional or advising week — and where is it missing? Not "I should reflect more," but: "The moment where I could reflect is ____, and right now I ____ instead."

FacilitatorLet pairs work without interruption. Move through the room and listen. The close will be richer if you've heard two or three specific answers while circulating — you can name them without attributing.

Close · 5 min
5 min5:00
Whole group close
One sentence per table, using the frame below.

Closing sentence frame: "One moment in my week where reflection could live — and currently doesn't — is ____."

FacilitatorNo commentary after each table. After all tables speak, name the pattern you heard and connect it to the campus focus. You close last — name one thing you will do differently based on today's session. Model it.

Watch-for: If someone raises "that's a call center, not a classroom" — take it seriously. Ask: "What would the equivalent 15 minutes look like at the end of a lab session or a clinical rotation? Does that moment exist anywhere in your current week?" Several department planning templates already include a reflection field. That's your evidence it's possible here.
